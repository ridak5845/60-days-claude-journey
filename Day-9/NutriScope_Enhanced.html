Nutrition Sources
Data sources and references used in NutriScope.
Nutritional Data References
1
ICMR-NIN Nutritive Value of Indian Foods
Indian Council of Medical Research / National Institute of Nutrition. The primary reference for food composition data of Indian foods used in this database. Values are per 100g edible portion.
2
USDA FoodData Central
United States Department of Agriculture. Used to supplement micronutrient values (vitamins D, B12) where ICMR data is limited. Available at fdc.nal.usda.gov
3
ICMR Dietary Reference Intakes (DRI) — 2020
Recommended Dietary Allowances (RDA) for Indians. Used to set daily nutrient targets for protein, iron, calcium, vitamins, and minerals. Values vary by age, gender, and physiological state.
4
Mifflin-St Jeor Equation for BMR
Mifflin MD, et al. (1990). "A new predictive equation for resting energy expenditure in healthy individuals." Am J Clin Nutr. Used to calculate Basal Metabolic Rate (BMR), which is multiplied by an activity factor to estimate TDEE.
5
WHO Global Targets for Non-communicable Diseases
World Health Organization guidelines on sodium intake (≤2000mg/day), sugar limits, and saturated fat recommendations used as the basis for the Risk Analysis module.
6
FSSAI (Food Safety and Standards Authority of India)
Reference for food labelling standards and recommended daily values used for packaged food interpretation in India. Useful for validating per-serving nutritional data.
Methodology Notes

• Serving sizes are approximate standard portions used in everyday Indian meals (e.g., 1 serving of Rice = 150g cooked).

• Micronutrient targets use conservative RDA estimates. Some nutrients like Vitamin D are notoriously hard to meet through diet alone — sunlight exposure is a primary source.

• Bioavailability is not accounted for — plant-based iron (non-haem) absorbs at 2–20%, while animal-based (haem) iron absorbs at 15–35%. Actual iron status depends on absorption factors including Vitamin C co-consumption.

• All values are estimates. Real nutrient content varies by variety, ripeness, cooking method, soil composition, and storage. Use this app as a directional guide, not a clinical measurement.
