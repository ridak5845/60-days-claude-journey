**60 Days Claude AI Challenge**

**Day 7: Building My Ideal Claude Workflow**

Overview

On Day 7 of my 60 Days Claude AI Challenge, I learnt something that many AI users overlook: how to use Claude effectively instead of simply using it more often.

I understood how different Claude models and effort levels are designed to solve different types of problems. My goal was to create a personal workflow that helps me get better results while saving time and resources.

This exercise helped me realize that AI productivity is not just about writing better prompts—it's also about choosing the right model and reasoning depth for the task at hand.



What I Explored

I studied the differences between Claude's models and when each one should be used.

—>Haiku

Best for:

- Quick lookups
- Definitions
- Flashcards
- Simple Q&A
- Fast responses

Key characteristic:

- Fastest and most lightweight model
- Ideal when deep reasoning is not required

—>Sonnet

Best for:

- Coding
- Debugging
- Research
- Learning new concepts
- Writing and summarization

Key characteristic:

- Balanced combination of speed and reasoning
- Suitable for most daily tasks

—>Opus

Best for:

- Complex problem-solving
- Thesis writing
- System architecture
- Deep literature reviews
- High-stakes projects

Key characteristic:

- Strongest reasoning capabilities
- Best reserved for tasks that genuinely require extensive analysis



—>Understanding Effort Levels

Another important concept I learned was choosing the appropriate effort level.

Low Effort

Use when:

- Asking quick questions
- Rewriting sentences
- Getting simple explanations

Standard Effort

Use when:

- Coding
- Research summaries
- Study assistance
- Everyday productivity tasks

High Effort

Use when:

- Writing assignments
- Solving difficult bugs
- Creating detailed explanations
- Generating higher-quality outputs

Max Effort

Use when:

- Working on a thesis
- Designing complex systems
- Conducting deep research
- Solving highly complex problems



—>My Personal Claude Workflow

After today's learning, I created a workflow that I plan to follow:

Task| Model| Effort Level
Quick Questions| Haiku| Low
Flashcards & Study Notes| Haiku| Low
Coding & Debugging| Sonnet| Standard
Learning New Topics| Sonnet| Standard
Research Summaries| Sonnet| Standard
Assignment Drafts| Sonnet| High
System Design| Opus| Max
Thesis & Deep Research| Opus| Max



—>Key Takeaways

1. The strongest model isn't always the best choice

Initially, I thought using the most powerful model for every task would automatically produce the best results.

Today I learned that efficiency comes from matching the model to the complexity of the task.



2. AI workflows matter as much as prompts

Prompt engineering is important, but selecting the correct model and effort level can have an equally large impact on output quality.



3. Most daily work does not require maximum reasoning

Many common tasks can be handled effectively with balanced models and standard effort levels.

This saves time while still producing high-quality results.



4. Reserve advanced reasoning for high-value work

Complex projects such as dissertations, architecture design, and deep research benefit significantly from stronger models and higher effort levels.



5. Iteration is part of the process

One prompt rarely produces the perfect answer.

The best results come from refining prompts, asking follow-up questions, and treating Claude as a collaborative thinking partner.



—>What I Learned

Today's lesson changed the way I think about AI productivity.

Before this exercise, I focused primarily on crafting better prompts. After studying Claude's workflow strategy, I realized that productivity with AI is a combination of:

- Choosing the right model
- Choosing the right effort level
- Writing clear prompts
- Iterating on responses

The biggest insight was that effective AI usage is not about maximizing power—it is about optimizing decision-making.

By using the right model for the right task, I can work faster, learn more efficiently, and achieve better outcomes without unnecessary complexity.
