Before We Begin

You won't be quizzed. You'll be shown.

Every day you scroll past headlines built to make you click and posts built to make you feel something before you think. This isn't about memorizing rules — it's about noticing the small, repeatable tricks that show up everywhere, so you start seeing them automatically.

You'll read two short, entirely fictional pieces of media. For each one, you'll react first — the way you naturally would — and only afterward will the analysis reveal what was actually going on underneath.

Concept · Headline Accuracy

Headlines are written to be clicked, not to be accurate

A headline's job is to earn a click in under two seconds — so writers reach for absolute words ("proves," "destroys," "secret"), vague sourcing ("experts say"), and numbers stripped of context. The actual article underneath is often far more careful and far less exciting than its own headline.

Why it matters: outrage and curiosity spread faster than nuance. If you only read headlines — which most people do — you absorb the exaggerated version of the world, not the real one.

Concept · Emotional Manipulation

Feelings move faster than facts

Fear, anger, and awe all make you act — share, comment, buy — before you've evaluated whether the claim is even true. Posts engineered for reach lean hard on emotional trigger words and urgency, often at the expense of specifics.

Why it matters: noticing your own emotional reaction as it happens — "wait, why do I suddenly feel afraid?" — is the single fastest way to catch manipulation in the moment, before you share it.
