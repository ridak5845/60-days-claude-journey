## Privacy Insights

- My Digital Footprint Score was **74/100**, showing a significant online presence.
- My Privacy Score was **38/100**, indicating room for improvement.
- Google and Meta together accounted for a large share of the estimated exposure.
- Behavioral tracking and social connections were the biggest exposure areas.


## Risk Analysis

### High-Risk Areas
- Social media activity
- Behavioral profiling
- Device tracking

### Medium-Risk Areas
- Location sharing
- Payment-related data
- Communication metadata


## Key Learnings

- Privacy is influenced by entire ecosystems, not just individual apps.
- A few companies can collect signals from multiple services at once.
- Behavioral data is one of the most valuable forms of user data.
- Small privacy setting changes can significantly improve overall privacy posture.
- AI can turn simple app lists into meaningful privacy insights.


## What Surprised Me Most

I was surprised to learn that even though I use 15 different apps, around **40% of my estimated exposure came from just two ecosystems: Google and Meta.** It showed how connected today's digital platforms really are.


## Conclusion

This project helped me better understand how digital footprints are created and how privacy exposure can be visualized through AI-powered dashboards. It was a great exercise in combining data analysis, cybersecurity concepts, and user-focused dashboard design.
